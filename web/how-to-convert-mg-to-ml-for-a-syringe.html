How do I convert mg or mcg to mL for a syringe?

Divide the amount you type by the concentration you type. The result is a volume in milliliters.

Units on an insulin syringe are that volume multiplied by the calibration printed on the barrel: times 100 for U-100, or times 40 for U-40. A U-100 count is not a U-40 count.

Your dose comes from your prescriber. This page only calculates with the numbers you type. It does not recommend a dose, diagnose, or interpret a lab result.

DoseTrace is for your own records. It is not for a healthcare professional dosing a patient.

Insulin-syringe units depend on the calibration printed on that syringe. U-100 means 100 units = 1 mL. U-40 means 40 units = 1 mL. Those counts are not interchangeable.

Example only

  1. 160 mg split into 2 equal parts is 80 mg for one part.
  2. 80 mg ÷ 250 mg/mL = 0.32 mL.
  3. 0.32 mL × 100 = 32 units on a U-100 insulin syringe only (100 units = 1 mL).
  4. 0.32 mL × 40 = 12.8 units on a U-40 insulin syringe only (40 units = 1 mL).

The 32-unit figure applies only to U-100. These numbers are an example, not a dose.

Limits

  • These pages do arithmetic only. They do not check whether any concentration, volume, or fraction is appropriate.
  • Blank, zero, and negative numbers are rejected. Division by zero is not performed.
  • A result that does not come out evenly is labeled rounded, and the exact fraction is shown beside it. Results are not rounded silently.
  • Milligrams and micrograms convert by a factor of 1000. IU is not a mass unit and is not converted here.
  • A comma followed by exactly three digits, such as 5,000, is read as five thousand. A dot, as in 5.000, is a decimal point, so that example is read as 5.
  • A real syringe or tablet may not have a mark at the exact result. This page does not move the result to the nearest mark.
