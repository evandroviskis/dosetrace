# i18n grammar pass — 2026-10-15

Founder request (2026-10-15): "corrija toda a gramática do aplicativo, seguindo uma linguagem natural, que não
seja muito formal, mas que passe claramente o que nós queremos que signifique." The trigger was the French Journey
food card line "1 jours d'affilée sur 7", fixed earlier today.

Scope: every user-facing string in `i18n/translations.js` (2,248 strings per language, FAQ included) in all 6
languages, plus `locales/*.json` (iOS/Android permission texts; reviewed, no change needed). I also searched
`screens/`, `components/` and `lib/` for counts and text joined in code. That search found one: BodyScreen glued a
number in front of "value(s) couldn't be read…". It is now a real `{count}` string with a singular form.
This pass builds on the native-meaning review (`docs/review/i18n-review-2026-10-15.md`) and keeps its terms:
frasco / vial / flacon / Fläschchen / flacone; angemischt; Seus números reais / Tus cifras reales / Vos vrais
chiffres / Deine echten Zahlen / I tuoi numeri reali; registro de comida / journal alimentaire /
Essensprotokoll / diario alimentare.

Register: es tú (Spain), pt você (Brazil), de du, it tu. **French keeps "vous" everywhere (founder decision
2026-10-15, recorded in docs/decisions.md)**. The pass found no stray "tu" form in French, and a guard test now
keeps it that way. No string gives advice, a diagnosis or an interpretation, and every placeholder is unchanged
(the parity test passes).

Method: each language was split in two halves and read line by line against the English by a native-level
reviewer. Each change was checked against the screen that uses it and against the approved-copy tests. Counts
were audited centrally: for every `{n}`/`{count}`/`{days}`/`{d}`/`{weeks}`/`{logged}` string I read the code
that fills it, to see whether it can be 1 (or 0 in French).

## Tests added (each failed on the old strings first)

- `__tests__/i18nGrammar.test.js`: class guards. No "(s)/(es)/(e)/(i)" plurals (French inclusive "(e)" allowed);
  French never uses "tu"; es/pt/de/it never use usted / o senhor / formal Sie / Lei; French elision; pt/es/it
  contractions (em o, a el, in il, including a screen name after "in"); no double, leading or trailing spaces; no
  English left in a translation; a sentence never starts with a `{day}` that can be a lowercase date; the 11 new
  singulars exist in 6 languages with the same placeholders, read right at 1 (French 0), and the screens pick them
  with `pluralKey`. Run against the old `translations.js`, 6 of these 11 failed. The 5 class guards (vous, register,
  elision, spaces, stray English) already passed: they hold classes the reviewers checked by hand.
- `__tests__/i18nGrammarFixes.test.js`: the 290 one-off fixes are pinned. Each row is a piece of the old wrong text,
  and the current string must not contain it. This blocks the error from coming back without freezing the wording.
- Updated: `todayLook.test.js` (Today passes `language` to the doses label) and `gateBFindings.test.js` (its mock
  `t` has the new `_one` key).

## Open questions for the founder

1. **English serial comma**: the app mixes "labs, vaccine cards, and vials" with "labs, vaccine cards and vials".
   Pick one house style? (Nothing was changed.)
2. **Terms of service / FAQ "Can I cancel anytime?"** only say how to cancel through the Apple ID. Should a Google
   Play line be added for Android users? (This is content, not grammar, so it was left unchanged.)
3. **Reality check "three" or "four" numbers?** cal_rc_sub says "Three numbers you already know", but
   cal_rc_locked_lead says "four numbers you already have" for the same check. Which is right?
4. **German "healthcare provider"**: the AI strings say "medizinische Fachperson" (pinned by AP-35). Six other
   strings say "medizinische Fachkraft". Should one term be used across the app?
5. **German ester names** ("Testosteron Cypionat"): standard German writes "Testosteron-Cypionat". Compound names
   are protected (search and matching), so nothing was changed. Hyphenate all rtu_* names?
6. **Portuguese "do {store}"** in paywall_cancel_sub_msg reads right for Google Play. For iPhone, Apple writes
   "a App Store". Is it acceptable as is?

Known edges left (rare, listed so they are not lost): notif_vial_body_one/_other in French at 0 doses ("0 doses");
ap_range_*_one when the range is 1 ("1 fois"); ap_fact_vial / ap_row_lasts_value when a vial lasts about 1 day.
Several keys with count wording have no screen at all (vax_review_note, blood_review_found_*, nutri_avg_days,
nutri_entries_count, protocols_injections_left, vials_days_of_total, nutri_tot_week/window, cal_rc_use_log,
cal_rc_elapsed, nutri_run_working). They were left as dead keys.

# Follow-up 2026-10-15: the open questions, decided and applied

The six open questions above were decided from the decisions ledger and from logic, not put to the founder. All
of them are applied below, test first: 7 new guards were added to `__tests__/i18nGrammar.test.js`, and all 7
failed before the fix. Result: 69 strings changed (en 40, es 4, pt 7, fr 4, de 10, it 4) and 12 keys added.

1. **English serial (Oxford) comma everywhere.** It now appears in 41 short lists in app strings and in 13 lists in
   the privacy policy (punctuation only, no word changed). The new guard flags any short "A, B and C" list. The
   food-chat example "3 eggs, 2 slices of toast and a coffee" is the one exception, on purpose: it is what a user
   types, and the demo animation splits it on ", " and " and ". Seven approved-copy tests pinned the old English, and
   each pin was updated to the serial-comma text (no other change): featureExplainers (recon and energy explainers),
   progressOneCard (cal_need_inputs, cal_numbers_prompt), protocolAssistant AP-4 (the per day / week / month
   question), scanCopy (COMBINED), trashPurge (purge body), and weighInEdit (cal_wi_del_body).
2. **Android cancelling.** The Terms (settings_terms_body) and the FAQ answer to "Can I cancel anytime?" now name
   Google Play as well as the Apple ID, in all 6 languages, using Google Play's own menu names: Payments &
   subscriptions › Subscriptions / Pagos y suscripciones › Suscripciones / Pagamentos e assinaturas › Assinaturas /
   Paiements et abonnements › Abonnements / Zahlungen und Abos › Abos / Pagamenti e abbonamenti › Abbonamenti. No
   test pinned the old Terms or FAQ text, so no pin had to change. I also changed English "cancelled" to "canceled"
   in the Terms, and the German FAQ now writes "Apple-ID".
3. **Reality check, "three" or "four" numbers.** The check uses three numbers the user gives: the weight at the
   start, the weight at the end, and the average daily intake (from the food log or typed in). The number of days
   comes from the dates. The start sheet (CalculatorSection, rcStartSheet) asks for one weight, and the rest comes
   later. So **cal_rc_sub ("Three numbers…") was right, and cal_rc_locked_lead ("four numbers") was wrong**. It is now
   "three" in all 6 languages. No screen currently shows cal_rc_locked_lead, but it was fixed so it can't come back
   wrong.
4. **German "medizinische Fachperson" everywhere.** "Fachkraft" was replaced in 6 strings: privacy policy,
   disclaimer, body hub footnote, vaccine disclaimer, lab hub disclaimer, and protocols_calc_disclaimer. Both words
   are feminine, so no ending changed. The guard: no "Fachkraft" in German.
5. **German ester hyphenation**: left as it is (compound names feed matching).
6. **{store} and articles.** {store} is "Apple ID" or "Google Play" (lib/paywallPlans storeName). In Portuguese,
   "do {store}" had to agree with whichever name came in, so the three strings now say "nas assinaturas da sua conta
   {store}": the article agrees with "conta". I checked every other language: es "de {store}", fr "compte {store}" /
   "abonnement {store}", it "di {store}" / "account {store}", and de "{store}-Konto" never put an article right
   before {store}. The new guard keeps it that way for es/pt/fr/it.

Rare edges:
- **Vial-low notification in French at 0**: notif_vial_body_one now carries {n} in all 6 languages, and both call
  sites in lib/notifications.js pick it with `isOne(remaining, t.language)`, so French reads "Il reste 0 dose".
- **"1 fois" when the frequency range is 1**: no change was needed. In `spacingOptions`, lo === hi === 1 only
  happens when the option is exact (n = 1). The exact branch uses "Every day" / "Every 7 days", so
  ap_range_*_one never gets 1. A range like "1 or 2 times" reads fine in every language.
